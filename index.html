Exam habits

Practice questions that only ask for a definition are much easier than the real exam, so a high score on them can give false confidence. The bank here has been rewritten in the real formats: situations, “which two statements”, missing words, and wrong answers that sound almost right. Use it to learn the reasoning, not to memorise answers.

Exam habitWhy it matters
Answer the ITIL wayYou have real experience. When your job does it differently, the exam still wants the ITIL answer.
Underline NOT, MOSTLY, FIRST, CORRECTOne word changes the answer. Read the question twice.
Remove two options firstUsually two are clearly wrong. Then compare the last two word by word.
“Which two” questionsDecide on each statement alone (true or false), then find the option that matches.
Watch for swapped definitionsA wrong answer is often the exact definition of a neighbouring term (deployment vs release, problem vs known error).
About 90 seconds eachFlag hard questions and come back. Never leave one blank: there is no penalty for guessing.
